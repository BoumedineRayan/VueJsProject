Great White Shark

Great White Shark Facts

  • Length: 3.6-6 meters (12-20 feet), with some individuals reported up to 7 meters (23 feet)
  • Weight: 680-1,800 kg (1,500-4,000 lbs)
  • Lifespan: 30-70 years
  • Diet: Fish, rays, other sharks, seals, sea lions, dolphins, whale carcasses
  • Habitat: Coastal and offshore waters in temperate and subtropical regions worldwide
  • Behavior: Solitary hunters but can aggregate at food sources; known for breaching behavior when hunting seals
  • Reproduction: Ovoviviparous; females give birth to 2-10 pups after a gestation period of about 12 months
  • Conservation status: Vulnerable (IUCN Red List)
  • Population: Decreasing due to human activities

Why are they endangered?

Great white sharks are classified as vulnerable due to several human-induced threats. They are often caught as bycatch in commercial fishing gear, particularly in longlines and gillnets intended for other species. Illegal hunting continues despite protection in many countries, as their fins, teeth, and jaws are highly valued in the market. Negative public perception, fueled by media portrayal, has led to targeted killings and shark culling programs. The decline in their food sources due to overfishing also affects their survival. Ocean pollution and habitat degradation further threaten these apex predators. Climate change is affecting ocean temperatures and prey distribution, potentially altering their migration patterns and hunting grounds. Conservation efforts include fishing regulations, marine protected areas, and public education to improve the public perception of sharks.
