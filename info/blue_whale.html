Blue Whale

Blue Whale Facts

  • Length: Up to 30 meters (98 feet)
  • Weight: Up to 200 tons (400,000 lbs)
  • Lifespan: 80-90 years
  • Diet: Almost exclusively krill; can eat up to 4 tons of krill per day
  • Habitat: Found in all oceans except the Arctic; they migrate between cold-water feeding grounds and warm-water breeding grounds
  • Behavior: Generally solitary or travel in pairs; known for their powerful, low-frequency calls that can be heard hundreds of miles away
  • Reproduction: Females give birth to a single calf every 2-3 years after a gestation period of 10-12 months
  • Conservation status: Endangered (IUCN Red List)
  • Population: Estimated 10,000-25,000 individuals worldwide, a small fraction of their pre-whaling population

Why are they endangered?

The blue whale was driven to the brink of extinction by commercial whaling in the 20th century. Although whaling is now banned, they still face serious threats. Ship strikes are a major cause of mortality, as their migration routes often cross major shipping lanes. Entanglement in fishing gear can injure or drown them. Ocean noise pollution from ships and military sonar interferes with their communication, which is vital for finding mates and food. Climate change is a growing threat, as it affects the abundance and distribution of krill, their primary food source. Like other marine animals, they are also vulnerable to pollution and the ingestion of microplastics. While their populations are slowly recovering, these modern threats continue to hinder their comeback.
