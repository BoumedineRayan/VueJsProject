Sea Turtle

Sea Turtle Facts

  • Length: 60-190 cm (2-6 ft), depending on species
  • Weight: 70-700 kg (155-1,540 lbs), depending on species
  • Lifespan: 50-80 years or more
  • Diet: Varies by species; includes seagrass, algae, jellyfish, crabs, and shrimp
  • Habitat: Found in warm and temperate seas worldwide; they nest on sandy beaches
  • Behavior: Migrate long distances between feeding grounds and nesting beaches; females return to the same beach where they were born to lay eggs
  • Reproduction: Females lay 50-200 eggs per nest and may nest multiple times in a season
  • Conservation status: Most species are threatened or endangered (IUCN Red List)
  • Population: All species have declining populations due to human threats

Why are they endangered?

Nearly all species of sea turtle are classified as threatened or endangered. They face a multitude of dangers, many of which are caused by humans. Bycatch in commercial fishing gear is one of the greatest threats, where turtles are accidentally caught in nets, longlines, and trawlers. Habitat loss is another critical issue; coastal development and artificial lighting disrupt nesting sites and disorient hatchlings. Climate change impacts sand temperatures, which determines the sex of hatchlings, and rising sea levels flood nests. Pollution, especially plastic debris, is often mistaken for food like jellyfish, leading to internal injury. Finally, illegal poaching of turtles and their eggs for meat, leather, and shells continues to threaten their survival. Conservation efforts focus on protecting nesting beaches, modifying fishing gear, and reducing pollution.
