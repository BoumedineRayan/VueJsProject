Dolphin

Dolphin Facts

  • Length: 1.7-4 meters (5.5-13 feet), depending on species
  • Weight: 50-200 kg (110-440 lbs), depending on species
  • Lifespan: 20-45 years in the wild
  • Diet: Fish, squid, and crustaceans
  • Habitat: Found in oceans and some rivers worldwide, from tropical to temperate waters
  • Behavior: Highly intelligent and social; live in pods; communicate through clicks and whistles
  • Reproduction: Gestation period of 9-17 months depending on species; usually give birth to one calf
  • Conservation status: Varies by species - some are endangered, others are of least concern (IUCN Red List)
  • Population: Some species declining due to human activities

Why are they endangered?

Several dolphin species are endangered due to various human activities. Bycatch in fishing nets is a major threat, as dolphins often get trapped and drown in fishing gear intended for other species. Ocean pollution, including plastic waste and chemical contaminants, affects dolphin health and food sources. Habitat degradation from coastal development, shipping traffic, and noise pollution disrupts dolphin communication and navigation. Climate change impacts ocean temperatures and food availability, affecting dolphin populations. Some species are also hunted for food or captured for entertainment purposes. Conservation efforts include creating marine protected areas, implementing fishing regulations to reduce bycatch, reducing ocean pollution, and international agreements to protect dolphin habitats.
